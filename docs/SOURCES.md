# Annotated sources and evidence map

**Targeted Albania source review:** 2026-10-20; other cases retain the 2026-10-18 review. This is an initial list, not a systematic literature review. URLs below match the pilot CSV. Case coding is *provisional*, and sources often document only part of the causal story.

## Scholarly foundation

- **Chenoweth & Shay, 2022 — NAVCO 2.1:** https://doi.org/10.1177/00223433221092938 — study design, campaign-year measures, historical trends, limitations.
- **NAVCO 2.1 dataset and codebook:** https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/MHOXDV — systematic maximalist-campaign data for a future stage; *not copied into this repository*.
- **Shahini, 2021 — Albanian student movement of December 1990:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpos.2021.708881 — historical and sociological case study, including university organization and regime response.
- **Zekthi, 2025 — Albanian student protests and political outcomes:** https://doi.org/10.36941/jesr-2025-0138 — qualitative assessment of the limited durable effects of 2018–2019 mobilization.

## Case-level evidence

### Albania 1990–1992 (ALB-1990)
- Arjan Shahini, *Frontiers in Political Science* (2021): https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/political-science/articles/10.3389/fpos.2021.708881/full — detailed chronology and structural context. **Interpretive scholarly analysis, not a complete account of every actor.**

### Albania 1997 (ALB-1997)
- Christopher Jarvis, *IMF Finance & Development* (2000): https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/fandd/2000/03/jarvis.htm — pyramid schemes, violence, collapse of state authority and eventual elections.
- IMF Working Paper (1999): https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/1999/098/article-A001-en.xml — deeper historical/economic context. **Do not code armed crisis as a nonviolent victory.**

### Albania 2018–2019 (ALB-2018)
- Prime Minister's Office, *Pact for the University* announcement (2018): https://www.kryeministria.al/en/newsroom/mbi-dokumentin-e-punes-mbi-paktin-per-universitetin/ — **government's description of commitments**, not independent verification.
- Fitimtar Zekthi (2025): https://www.richtmann.org/journal/index.php/jesr/article/view/14352 — later academic evaluation of political impact and limited sustained institutional change.

### Albania 2026 (ALB-2026)
- **Current source ledger:** [20 October review](ALBANIA_REVIEW_2026-10-20.md), including Reuters (17 October), AP (20 October), Le Monde (18 October, updated 19 October) and Al Jazeera (18 October). It separates observations, official statements, allegations and coding inference.
- **Coding:** `uncertain` / `ongoing` / `unresolved`. Incident-level violence is supported; campaign-wide predominance is not established. See the review for the alternative narrower-wave coding.
- **Earlier citation retained for provenance:** Reuters, 17 October, via [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/albanian-antigovernment-protesters-clash-with-police-in-tirana-4955402). The current CSV uses an accessible WTAQ republication of the same Reuters report, not an independent corroboration.
- **Historical comparison:** the review also adds contemporary Nature coverage and OBCT material to the 2018 government announcement and Zekthi retrospective. It distinguishes policy announcements from verified delivery.

### Serbia 2000 (SRB-2000)
- OSCE/ODIHR, September 2000: https://odihr.osce.org/odihr/elections/52919 — contemporary disputes about vote reporting.
- Binnendijk & Marović, *Communist and Post-Communist Studies* (2006): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0967067X06000250 — scholarly account of security-force responses in Serbia and Ukraine.

### Armenia 2018 (ARM-2018)
- *The Guardian*, April 23, 2018: https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/apr/23/serzh-sargsyan-resigns-as-armenias-prime-minister-after-protests — contemporary report of Sargsyan's resignation.
- *The Guardian*, April 25, 2018: https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/apr/25/armenia-calls-for-new-rally-after-talks-on-replacing-prime-minister-halted — post-resignation challenges. **Leader exit did not instantly conclude every contested demand.**

### Tunisia 2010–2011 (TUN-2011)
- Freedom House, *Freedom in the World 2022*: https://freedomhouse.org/country/tunisia/freedom-world/2022 — 2011 democratic opening and 2021 executive consolidation.
- Freedom House, *Freedom in the World 2026*: https://freedomhouse.org/country/tunisia/freedom-world/2026 — later institutional trajectory. **An eventual reversal is distinct from the immediate 2011 result.**

### Bangladesh 2024 (BGD-2024)
- United Nations / OHCHR, February 12, 2025: https://bangladesh.un.org/en/289108-bangladesh-un-report-finds-brutal-systematic-repression-protests-calls-justice-serious — documented crackdown and evidence basis.
- Human Rights Watch, *World Report 2025*: https://www.hrw.org/world-report/2025/country-chapters/bangladesh — government resignation and interim arrangements. **State repression and protester-related violence need separate codes.**

## Important biases and revisions

A source covering a dramatic protest may overrepresent violent events; retrospective reports can reinterpret earlier events based on later outcomes. Government announcements show commitments, **not** verified implementation. NGO reports, journal articles and international agency analyses also have scopes and methodological limits. Every row needs independent review before academic claims.
