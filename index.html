01 / THE RESEARCH QUESTION

What does it actually mean
for a protest to work?

“Success” cannot be reduced to one photograph, one resignation, or one election. This project separates four outcomes that are too often confused.

01

Policy change

03 / HYPOTHESES, NOT VERDICTS

The patterns we
want to test.

These are research questions derived from comparative political science. The small, selected pilot sample does not establish cause and effect.

H / 01

Does the state stay united?

Compare outcomes when political elites and security institutions remain cohesive versus when they fracture.

↗
H / 02

What happens after a concession?

Investigate whether announced reforms become lasting policy — or leave the underlying grievance unresolved.

↗
H / 03

Can a leader fall while the system survives?

Distinguish immediate leadership turnover from deeper institutional changes and democratic durability.

↗
H / 04

Why can major movements end differently?

Study movement goals, organization, repression and the political context — not just the size of crowds.

05 / HOW TO READ THIS PROJECT

Evidence first.
Certainty last.

This is an open, exploratory research project — not a completed political-science paper. The cases were selected to illuminate different outcomes, not to measure the worldwide success rate of civil resistance.

Every case contains a documented result, an important limitation, and links to its sources. Ongoing movements remain unresolved. Mixed or armed unrest is distinguished from predominantly nonviolent resistance.
